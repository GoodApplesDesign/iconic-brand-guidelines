Monochrome

Most of what we make should be Noir and Blanc. The contrast is confident and timeless, and it lets the metals do the talking.

Graphite is the supporting neutral, for secondary surfaces, cards and dividers.

Colour alternates

Brown and Olive add warmth and richness alongside monochrome. Use them as feature backgrounds, each paired with its own metal: Bronze on Brown, Gold on Olive.

Brown is a rich, bronze-toned brown. Olive is a true, distinct olive, never muted or dull. Use one alternate at a time, and never set them against each other.

Metals

Silver, Bronze and Gold share one gradient structure: a soft highlight, a deeper shadow and smooth transitions, so the three read as a set. A Graphite gradient carries the ring on Blanc.

The gradients belong to the ring. Use them for the logo and for small accents such as rules and icons, never as large fills or behind text.

Gradient settings

All four metals use the same stops, so they can be swapped for one another. In Illustrator, apply them as linear gradients at −55°. On screen, use the CSS equivalent.

  • TypeLinear
  • Stops0, 18, 36, 46, 56, 78, 100%
  • Angle−55° in Illustrator, 125deg in CSS
  • Illustrator swatchesV3 Gold, V3 Bronze, V3 Silver, V3 Graphite

Incorrect usage

Keep the palette tight. Avoid these.

Large gold fill
Don’tuse the metal gradients as backgrounds or large fills
Gradient type
Don’tset text in a gradient
Don’tput Brown and Olive side by side
Don’tintroduce colours outside the palette, red included
Don’tuse tints or transparencies of the alternates
Small Bronze Lite body copy on Brown is hard to read.
Don’tset body copy in low-contrast pairings
